Executive summary

Open with the conclusion. State what happened this quarter in one paragraph, in the same words you would use out loud. A reader who stops here should still know the decision you are recommending and why.

Follow with the qualification: what the numbers do not yet show, and which of last quarter's assumptions turned out to be wrong. Naming the miss early buys credibility for everything after it.

What we learned

First finding, written as a claim

Support the claim with the specific evidence behind it, then say how confident you are. Where a number is directional rather than significant, label it as such — readers will trust the significant ones more.

Second finding

Keep each finding to a single idea. If a paragraph needs a chart to make sense, put the chart directly under it rather than in an appendix.

A verbatim customer quote earns more attention than a paraphrase, so use the customer's own words.

Recommendations

  1. Lead with the decision. Say what should happen, who owns it, and by when. Vague recommendations get deferred.
  2. Attach a cost. Headcount, quarters, or dollars — a recommendation without a price is a wish.
  3. Name the trade. What gets dropped to make room, stated plainly.
